Summary of work from yesterday, 2026-05-06:

Yesterday we focused on checking whether the posterior distributions, especially the
mutation-rate posteriors, could be affected by a scaling issue in the SLiM/ABC
pipeline. We reviewed how parameters are stored in the prior file, how they are
scaled inside SLiM, and how they are plotted in the prior/posterior density figures.

Key scaling notes so far:

- The prior CSV stores biological-scale parameters: gmu, imu, gd, id, gdfe, and idfe.
- SLiM scales mutation rates, selection coefficients, and recombination internally by
  Q during simulation.
- The ABC scripts use the original biological-scale parameter file for inference.
- The prior/posterior density figures plot biological-scale ABC parameter values, not
  SLiM-scaled values.
- The plotting code uses visual axis transformations only, such as log10 mutation-rate
  axes and absolute-value/log-style transforms for selection coefficients.
- We did not find evidence that the density plots are being distorted by an internal
  arithmetic error such as applying Q twice, plotting scaled values by accident, or
  mixing scaled and unscaled parameters.

Current multi-pronged analysis:

1. Scaling issue check:
   We are finishing an analysis to determine whether inferred posterior distributions
   are affected by the Q scaling scheme or by the interaction between Q scaling and
   strong deleterious selection coefficients.

2. New chrom5 Q25 simulation set:
   We are completing a new set of 100,000 chromosome 5 simulations with Q25. The goal
   is to test whether using a smaller Q changes the posterior pattern, especially for
   mutation-rate inference and DFE parameters.

3. Cap analysis:
   For both ABC approaches, rejection ABC and ABC-RF, and for both chromosomes 1 and
   5, we are running a "cap analysis." In this analysis, simulations are removed if
   their DFE parameter would become less than -1 after scaling. In other words, we
   remove simulations where abs(gdfe) * Q > 1 or abs(idfe) * Q > 1, then rerun ABC.
   The purpose is to see whether removing simulations with impossible or capped
   scaled selection effects meaningfully changes the overall posterior pattern.

Current interpretation:

- If the capped/filtered analyses look similar to the original analyses, then the
  posterior patterns are probably not being driven by the selection-coefficient cap.
- If the capped/filtered analyses differ strongly, then the original posterior may be
  sensitive to simulations where Q scaling pushed deleterious effects beyond the
  interpretable SLiM range.
- If the new Q25 chrom5 simulations give a different result from Q50, then Q itself
  may be influencing the simulated summary statistics or the posterior inference.

Open questions to track:

- Why are inferred mutation-rate posteriors often one to two orders of magnitude
  higher than expected?
- Is recapitation affecting simulated diversity or the allele-frequency spectrum more
  than expected?
- Does neutral overlay correctly represent total versus deleterious mutation-rate
  components?
- Are observed and simulated summary statistics matched in sample size, chromosome
  interval definitions, and filtering?
- Do no-recap neutral-overlay controls produce meaningfully different rejection ABC
  or ABC-RF posteriors?

To-do item 5 pipeline brainstorm: possible causes of heightened inferred mutation
rates relative to empirical estimates.

Pipeline checked end to end:

1. Prior generation:
   The prior files store biological-scale gmu and imu, usually drawn log-uniformly
   from 1e-10 to 1e-7. This range includes empirical Arabidopsis-like rates but
   also extends more than an order of magnitude above them, so ABC can choose high
   mutation rates if high-SNP/high-pi simulations are closest to the observed target.

2. SLiM forward simulation:
   In models/ABCtrees.slim, SLiM simulates only the deleterious mutation component
   forward:
   gmu_del_sim = gmu * gd * Q
   imu_del_sim = imu * id * Q
   This is conceptually correct if gmu/imu are total biological mutation rates and
   the neutral component is added later. I did not see a direct Q-applied-twice
   error in SLiM itself. However, the scaled DFE can still indirectly affect inferred
   mutation rates by changing how many deleterious mutations survive, especially
   when gdfe * Q or idfe * Q is very negative and selection coefficients are capped
   at -1.

3. Post-SLiM Python step, highest-priority suspects:
   scripts/trees_to_vcf.py overlays neutral mutations after optional recapitation:
   gmu_neu = gmu * Q * (1 - gd)
   imu_neu = imu * Q * (1 - id)
   and calls msprime.mutate(..., keep=True). This is also conceptually correct under
   the current split model because the final tree sequence should contain both the
   forward-simulated deleterious component and the overlaid neutral component.
   However, this step is the most important place to audit because small mismatches
   here directly change simulated diversity.

   Main concerns in this step:
   - scripts/trees_to_vcf.sbatch and scripts/trees_vcf_tajima.sbatch default to
     CHROM_DIR=chrom1 but default GENE_CSV, INTERGENE_CSV, and L are chromosome 5
     values. If chromosome 1 jobs were run without overriding those environment
     variables, the neutral overlay would use chromosome 5 intervals and length on
     chromosome 1 tree sequences. That would distort region-specific mutation
     placement and total mutable length. This is the strongest concrete pipeline
     issue found so far.
   - Recapitation uses NE_RECAP=6000 and RECOMB_RECAP=2e-6 for Q50 runs. These are
     scaled values, matching NPOP=300000/50 and R_REAL*50, but this should be checked
     for every Q-specific run. For Q25, the analogous values should be NPOP=12000 and
     recombination 1e-6 unless there is a deliberate reason to use different values.
   - Neutral mutations are added after recapitation, so recapitated deep branches can
     add substantial neutral diversity. If recapitation parameters or generation/time
     units are off, the overlaid neutral component could make simulated SNP counts too
     high or alter the SFS, pushing ABC toward higher or lower mutation rates in a
     compensatory way.
   - The script keeps existing deleterious mutations when adding neutral mutations.
     That is expected, but a no-neutral-overlay, neutral-only-overlay, and no-recap
     control would help verify that the total realized mutation count is close to
     gmu * Q and imu * Q on the scaled trees.

4. Simulated summary statistics:
   scripts/calc_sim_stats.py preferentially reads derived tree sequences when they
   exist, then falls back to VCFs. That confirms the current simulated stats are
   coming from trees_derived, not the raw SLiM tree folder. The stat code samples
   1135 diploid individuals by default and folds the SFS in the sbatch wrapper.
   Potential issues to keep on the list:
   - The tree path uses variant.site.position + 1 before interval classification,
     while SLiM/tree-sequence positions are naturally 0-based and the interval CSVs
     appear to be used as SLiM coordinates. The VCF path uses POS directly. This
     could create a one-base coordinate mismatch between tree-based simulated stats
     and VCF-based observed stats. It is probably too small to explain an order of
     magnitude mutation-rate shift by itself, but it is easy to test.
   - The tree path counts a diploid accession as derived if either haplotype carries
     the alternate allele. This matches the VCF accession-level counting style used
     in count_accessions(), but it is not allele-count SFS. If the empirical estimate
     assumes per-haplotype diversity, this could change pi/theta scaling.
   - Simulated stats allow all nucleotide variants unless SNP_ONLY=1 is set. The
     current simstats sbatch default is SNP_ONLY=0, while observed 1001 stats use
     SNP_ONLY=1. If msprime/SLiM produces non-SNP nucleotide changes or multiallelic
     sites, observed and simulated filters may not be exactly matched.

5. Observed summary statistics:
   observed_1001_simstats.sbatch subsets the empirical VCF to chromosome 1 or 5,
   uses SNP_ONLY=1, allows incomplete calls with MIN_VCF_CALL_FRACTION=0.8, and
   samples 1135 accessions. Simulated tree-based stats generally have complete
   samples. Missing-data differences can affect singleton/SFS proportions and pi,
   causing the ABC distance to prefer simulations with mutation rates that compensate
   for filtering differences rather than true biological rates.

6. ABC/rejection and ABC-RF:
   The ABC scripts merge simulated summary statistics with the biological-scale
   prior parameters by ID. They do not multiply gmu or imu by Q during inference or
   plotting, so the high posteriors are not a simple plotting-scale artifact. The
   rejection ABC distance z-scores all non-constant stats and uses Euclidean distance.
   If observed SNP counts/pi/theta are outside or near the high end of the simulated
   distribution, the accepted set will naturally concentrate at high gmu/imu. The
   next diagnostic should compare observed values to the accepted and full simulated
   distributions for g_snps, i_snps, g_pi, i_pi, g_theta_w, i_theta_w, and the SFS
   bins.

Most likely explanations to test first:

1. Post-SLiM interval/length mismatch, especially chromosome 1 runs accidentally
   using chromosome 5 GENE_CSV/INTERGENE_CSV/L defaults in trees_to_vcf.sbatch.
2. Recapitation parameter mismatch across Q values, especially Q25 runs if NE_RECAP
   and RECOMB_RECAP were not changed from Q50 defaults.
3. Observed-versus-simulated stat filtering mismatch: SNP_ONLY, missing data,
   accession-level rather than allele-level SFS, and complete versus incomplete
   samples.
4. ABC is correctly reporting biological-scale high mutation rates because observed
   diversity/SNP summaries sit closest to high-mu simulations under the current
   summary-statistic set.
5. Selection/DFE scaling and capping alter the surviving deleterious load, causing
   mutation rates to compensate indirectly, even without direct Q arithmetic errors.

Simplest-case refinement:

The high inferred mutation-rate signal was already present in the original
chromosome 5-only analysis, before adding other chromosomes or appending chr1+chr5
simulation panels. Therefore, chromosome-mixing issues and chr1 interval-default
problems cannot explain the original high-mu result. They remain pipeline hazards,
but they are not the root cause of the simplest chrom5-only pattern.

For the chrom5-only rejection ABC run, the posterior summaries are:
- gmu median = 1.80e-8, mean = 2.13e-8
- imu median = 2.58e-8, mean = 2.89e-8
- gd median = 0.175
- id median = 0.220

Observed chrom5 summary statistics sit high relative to the chrom5 simulation panel:
- g_snps: observed 14038, simulated median 1899, observed percentile 0.922
- i_snps: observed 9426, simulated median 1069, observed percentile 0.913
- g_pi: observed 0.003264, simulated median 0.000321, observed percentile 0.994
- i_pi: observed 0.002831, simulated median 0.000324, observed percentile 0.969
- g_theta_w: observed 0.007727, simulated median 0.001045, observed percentile 0.922
- i_theta_w: observed 0.008942, simulated median 0.001014, observed percentile 0.913

This means the simplest chrom5-only result is likely driven by the observed target
having much more diversity than most simulations, especially for pi. Under the
current fixed demography, selfing, recapitation, and summary-statistic definitions,
ABC reaches high gmu/imu because those simulations are the ones that can approach
the observed diversity.

Most likely explanations for the simplest chrom5-only case:

1. The fixed demographic model may underproduce neutral diversity for the empirical
   1001 Genomes sample, forcing mutation-rate estimates upward.
2. The fixed selfing rate, Ne, or recapitated ancestry may not match the empirical
   diversity scale, again causing mu to compensate.
3. The empirical mutation-rate estimate may not be directly comparable to the ABC
   parameter if the empirical value is neutral/effectively neutral, lineage-specific,
   per-generation under a different generation-time assumption, or genome-wide rather
   than restricted to these intervals.
4. Observed and simulated pi/theta/SFS definitions may still differ even in chrom5:
   observed stats are from a SNP-only VCF with missing-data tolerance, while simulated
   stats are usually calculated from complete derived tree sequences.
5. Selection could indirectly push the posterior toward high total mutation rates
   paired with low gd/id: accepted chrom5 simulations have lower deleterious
   fractions than the prior, so much of the high total gmu/imu is effectively neutral
   overlay rather than forward-simulated deleterious mutation.

Population-structure task:

Population structure in the 1001 Genomes target is now a leading explanation for
the inflated observed diversity. The current simulations are effectively modeling a
single randomly mating population with fixed selfing and demography, while the real
1001 Genomes sample combines accessions from multiple geographic/genetic groups. If
the observed sample mixes divergent groups, pairwise diversity, SNP count, theta,
and the SFS can all become inflated relative to a single-population simulation.
ABC may then compensate by choosing high mutation rates.

Goal:
Create one or more structure-reduced observed target datasets and rerun the same
chrom5 ABC pipeline. If the inferred gmu/imu posterior shifts downward toward the
empirical mutation-rate estimate, that would support population structure as a major
cause of the original high-mu result.

Possible approaches:

1. Remove accessions likely to inflate between-population diversity.
   First concrete test: remove North American and Cape Verde accessions from the
   1001 Genomes VCF before calculating observed target statistics. These groups may
   add deep between-group differences that the simple simulation model cannot
   represent.

2. Use a geographically/genetically narrower sample.
   Instead of analyzing all 1135 accessions together, choose a relatively coherent
   subset, such as one major regional group. Then recalculate observed stats and
   downsample simulations to the same number of accessions.

3. Compare full-sample versus filtered-sample summary statistics before rerunning ABC.
   Key quantities to compare:
   - g_pi and i_pi
   - g_snps and i_snps
   - g_theta_w and i_theta_w
   - Tajima's D
   - folded SFS proportions
   If filtering removes structure, pi and SNP counts should drop, and the observed
   target should move closer to the center of the simulated distribution.

4. Run ABC on multiple observed targets:
   - full 1001 sample
   - no North America / no Cape Verde
   - one or more within-region subsets
   - possibly equal-size random subsamples from the full and filtered datasets
   This will show whether high inferred mu is robust or mainly driven by structured
   sampling.

5. Longer-term modeling option:
   Instead of filtering the observed data, add population structure to the simulation
   model or use a demography inferred from structured Arabidopsis data. This is more
   realistic but more work. The filtering analysis is the fastest diagnostic.
